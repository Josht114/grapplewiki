Both of the opponent's arms are trapped, one between your legs and one by your arm, usually from turtle. Their neck is unprotected.

## Key points

1. From top turtle, hook their near arm with your legs.
2. Roll or slide to their side, keeping the arm trapped.
3. Control their other arm with your own arm.
4. Attack the neck or extend the trapped arm.

## Principles

### Goal
- Keep both arms trapped and attack the neck, or extend the trapped arm.

### Base and posture
- Chest to their back at an angle, your hips beside their hips, so they can't turn into you.
- Legs locked (triangled or crossed) on their near arm, well above the elbow.
- When you roll through, stay tight: your hips go under theirs, not out to the side.

### Hands and arms
- Your far arm controls their other arm, wrapping it at the wrist or near the elbow.
- Free hand goes to the neck: choke, or pull their head to set the trapped arm straight.
- Gi: a collar grip gives a strong choke from here.

### Priorities
1. Lock their near arm in your legs.
2. Control their other arm.
3. Choke, or extend the trapped arm for an armlock.
4. If they free an arm, switch to the back or back to top turtle.

### Common mistakes
- **Leg trap too low:** they bend the elbow and pull the arm out.
- **Giving them room to turn in:** they come up on top.
- **Rushing the sub before the arms are controlled:** you lose the trap.
