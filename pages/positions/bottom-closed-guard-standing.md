name: Bottom closed guard (standing)

counterpart: top-closed-guard-standing

Opponent has stood up inside your closed guard. Break their posture or switch to an open guard sweep before they open your legs.

## Key points

1. Keep your ankles locked while you have grips.
2. Pull a sleeve or collar to break their posture.
3. Sweep from the ankles or hips while they stand.
4. If the guard opens, switch to an open guard.

## Principles

### Goal
- Break their posture or sweep them while they stand; switch to an open guard if the guard opens.

### Base and posture
- Ankles locked high on their back, hips off the mat, pulling them in with your legs.
- Stay connected to them: don't hang off their waist with no grips.

### Hands and arms
- Pull them forward by the sleeves or collar (gi), or the wrists or a collar tie (no-gi).
- Keep their hands off your hips and knees; that's how they pry the legs open.

### Frames
- When the guard opens, put your feet on their hips at once instead of lying under them.

### Priorities
1. Keep your ankles locked while you have grips.
2. Pull a sleeve or collar to break their posture.
3. Sweep from the ankles or hips while they stand.
4. If the guard opens, switch to an open guard.

### Common mistakes
- **Holding the guard with no grips:** they posture up and stack you.
- **Ankles low on their hips:** easy for them to open with a knee.
- **Staying flat when they lift you:** they stack or slam. Open the guard and put your feet on the hips instead.
