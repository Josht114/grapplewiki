---
name: Bottom lapel guard (worm)
side: bottom
counterpart: top-lapel-guard
---
Gi guards that use the opponent's own lapel, often wrapped around their lead leg (worm guard). Strong control of distance and the lead leg.

## Key points

1. Pull out the opponent's lapel.
2. Feed it around their lead leg and grip it.
3. Use the lapel grip to anchor and steer the leg.
4. Sweep or take the back when they lose balance.

## Principles

### Goal
- Use their own lapel to tie up their lead leg, then sweep or take the back when they lose balance.

### Base and posture
- On your back or hip, turned toward the wrapped leg, with your hips close to it.
- Your leg wrapped around their lead leg (worm guard) or a foot on their hip, keeping the angle.

### Hands and arms
- Pull out their lapel, feed it around their lead leg and grip it close to the leg.
- Other hand on a sleeve or collar to control their upper body.
- Gi only: this guard needs a lapel.

### Frames
- The wrapped leg and lapel are your frame: they keep their lead knee away from your chest.
- Free foot on their hip or biceps keeps distance.

### Priorities
1. Get the lapel out and wrapped around their lead leg.
2. Grip it tight to the leg and add a sleeve or collar grip.
3. Steer the leg with the lapel to off-balance them.
4. Sweep or take the back when they step to rebalance.

### Common mistakes
- **Gripping the lapel far from the leg:** it slips and they step out.
- **Flat on your back:** no angle, so the wrap has no pull.
- **Spending too long setting up the wrap:** they pass while your hands are busy.
