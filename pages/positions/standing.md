Both athletes on their feet, no one with a dominant grip or angle. Everything starts here: takedowns, guard pulls and grip fighting.

## Key points

1. Stance: feet staggered, knees bent, hands up and in front.
2. Win the grip fight before you attack.
3. Level change before you shoot; don't reach from far away.
4. Keep your head up and your hips under you.

## Principles

### Goal
- Win the grips and the angle, then take them down or pull guard on your terms. Don't get taken down or snapped into a front headlock.

### Base and posture
- Staggered stance, knees bent, hips under your shoulders, weight on the balls of your feet.
- Head up and in front of your hips; a bent-over posture gets snapped down.
- Move in small steps and never cross your feet.

### Hands and arms
- Hands up and in front, elbows in, so they can't get an underhook or a body lock.
- Fight for inside position first: your hands inside theirs, then grip.
- Gi: sleeve and lapel or collar grips; break their grips before you settle yours.
- No-gi: collar tie, wrist control, two-on-one and underhooks.

### Priorities
1. Stance and distance: stay out of reach until you have a grip.
2. Win the grip fight: control their hands or head before you attack.
3. Off-balance them (snap, pull, circle), then level change and shoot or trip.
4. If they shoot, sprawl hips back and head down on top of them.

### Common mistakes
- **Reaching from far away:** you lean forward and give them your arms and head.
- **Head down, hips back:** they snap you into a front headlock.
- **Shooting with no setup:** they see it and sprawl.
