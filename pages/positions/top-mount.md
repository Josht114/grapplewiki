Sitting on the opponent's torso with knees on the mat. One of the most dominant positions, with chokes and arm attacks available.

## Key points

1. Low mount: grapevine their legs and keep your chest heavy when they bridge.
2. High mount: walk your knees up into their armpits to set up arm attacks.
3. Post your hands wide when they bridge, then re-settle.
4. When they push on your chest, climb higher or attack the arm.

## Principles

### Goal
- Stay mounted, climb high when their elbows open, then isolate an arm or the neck.

### Base and posture
- Knees tight to their hips or armpits, hips low and heavy.
- Low mount: chest down, head next to theirs, feet hooked under their legs (grapevine) to kill the bridge.
- High mount: sit up with your hips forward and knees in their armpits.

### Hands and arms
- When they bridge, post a hand wide on the side they're bridging to.
- Low mount: underhook the head and one arm, or cross-face.
- Gi: deep cross-collar grips set up chokes and make them defend.

### Priorities
1. Stabilise: low mount or grapevine until they stop bridging.
2. Climb high when their elbows come off their ribs.
3. Attack: armbar (via S-mount), americana, choke.
4. If they turn to their side, follow to technical mount or the back.

### Common mistakes
- **Sitting tall before they're tired:** a bridge rolls you.
- **Loose knees and feet:** they elbow-knee escape to half guard.
- **Overcommitting to a sub without a post:** they bridge you off in the middle of it.
