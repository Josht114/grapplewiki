Chest-to-chest across the opponent with their legs passed. A stable pin and the launch point for mount, knee on belly and shoulder locks.

## Key points

1. Crossface: drive your shoulder across their jaw to turn their head away.
2. Win the far underhook so they can't turn into you.
3. Keep your hips low and your weight on your toes, not your knees.
4. Block the near hip with your knee or elbow to stop them shrimping.

## Principles

### Goal
- Hold them flat, kill their frames and advance to mount, knee on belly or a submission.

### Base and posture
- Chest on chest, hips low, weight on your toes rather than your knees.
- Legs sprawled back or knees wide; keep your hips away from their knees.
- Change base (to kesa, reverse kesa or north-south) to follow them rather than letting go.

### Hands and arms
- Crossface: near arm under their head, shoulder driven into their jaw so they look away.
- Far underhook, or hand blocking the far hip, so they can't turn in.
- Near elbow or knee blocks their near hip to stop the shrimp.
- Gi: far collar or belt grips anchor the pin.

### Priorities
1. Crossface and far underhook: flatten them.
2. Block the near hip and pin the near arm so their frames can't form.
3. Advance when they react: mount, knee on belly or north-south.
4. Attack the arms they frame with (americana, kimura, armbar).

### Common mistakes
- **Hips high or kneeling:** they get a knee in and recover guard.
- **No crossface:** they turn into you and come up or recover guard.
- **Chasing a sub before the pin is settled:** they escape in the transition.
