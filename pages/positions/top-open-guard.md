You are passing an open guard, either standing or on your knees. Standing favours speed passes like the toreando; kneeling favours pressure passes like the knee cut, over-under and smash pass.

## Key points

1. Neutralise the feet and grips before you try to pass.
2. Standing: control the knees or pants and use speed and angles.
3. Kneeling: get your hips heavy and pin their hips and head.
4. Finish every pass chest to chest in side control.

## Principles

### Goal
- Get past their legs to a chest-to-chest pin.

### Base and posture
- Posture up, head up, hips back out of reach of their feet.
- Standing: staggered stance, knees bent. Kneeling: one knee down, the other foot posted.
- Keep your weight centred; don't lean into their feet.

### Hands and arms
- Break their grips before you pass; don't let them keep two points of control.
- Control the legs: knees, shins or ankles, and push them to one side.
- Gi: pants grips at the knees or ankles. No-gi: grip behind the knees or the ankles.
- Elbows in to block feet in the biceps.

### Priorities
1. Break their grips and hooks.
2. Control both legs or pin one.
3. Get an angle and beat the legs past one side.
4. Chest to chest: settle side control before you do anything else.

### Common mistakes
- **Leaning forward:** triangle or a sweep.
- **Square in front of them:** they get both feet in and keep guard.
- **Passing without grips:** they recover guard as soon as you move.
