Opponent holds your cross collar and a cuff or bicep grip. Break or neutralise the collar grip before passing.

## Principles

### Goal
- Break or neutralise the cross-collar grip before you pass. While it's on, every pass gives them a sweep or your back.

### Base and posture
- Posture up, head up, hips back; don't let the collar pull your head below your hips.
- Keep the leg on the collar-grip side back, so they can't put a foot on that hip and spin under you.

### Hands and arms
- Strip the collar grip first: two hands on the gripping wrist, pull it off your collar and down to their belly.
- Free the sleeve or biceps they're holding by circling your hand out or pulling your elbow back to your hip.
- Gi only: this guard needs a collar.

### Priorities
1. Posture up so the collar can't pull you forward.
2. Strip the collar grip, then the sleeve.
3. Control the legs (pants at the knees) and pass to the side away from where the collar grip was.

### Common mistakes
- **Passing with the collar grip still on:** they pull you over and take your back.
- **Letting them pull your head down:** that's where their sweeps and back takes start.
- **Leaving the sleeve:** with the sleeve and collar both held, they control both your arm and your posture.
