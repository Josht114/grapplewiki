Opponent is mounted on you. Keep elbows tight, protect the neck, and escape with bridges and elbow-knee movement.

## Key points

1. Keep your elbows tight to your ribs.
2. Protect your neck with your hands and chin.
3. Bridge first, then shrimp; one sets up the other.
4. Never turn your back to escape.

## Principles

### Goal
- Keep your elbows and neck safe, then bridge them off or elbow-knee escape back to guard.

### Base and posture
- Elbows tight to your ribs, chin tucked, feet flat on the mat close to your hips.
- Keep them low on your hips; once their knees reach your armpits, escapes get much harder.

### Hands and arms
- Hands and forearms on their hips or knees, elbows inside their knees.
- Hands near your neck when they reach for collars or the head.
- Gi: protect your collar and keep your lapel closed.

### Frames
- Forearms on their hips stop them climbing high and make room for the elbow-knee escape.
- Never frame on their chest with straight arms.

### Priorities
1. Elbows in, neck protected; stop them climbing.
2. Trap an arm and the foot on the same side, then bridge over that shoulder (upa).
3. If they post wide, elbow-knee escape to half guard or full guard.
4. Never turn your back to get out.

### Common mistakes
- **Pushing their chest:** they armbar you from S-mount.
- **Turning to your stomach:** you give them the back.
- **Bridging straight up without trapping a side:** they just post and ride it.
