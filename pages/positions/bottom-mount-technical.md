You turned to your side under mount and their knee is now behind your back. Turning further gives up your back, so turn back in and escape.

## Key points

1. Stop turning away: that gives them your back.
2. Turn back flat toward their posted leg.
3. Frame on the posted knee.
4. Elbow-knee escape to recover half guard.

## Principles

### Goal
- Turn back in and escape before they take your back or the arm.

### Base and posture
- Stop turning away; turn your shoulders back toward the mat.
- Turn your hips toward their posted leg, not away from it.

### Hands and arms
- Top arm tight to your ribs and head; that's the armbar arm.
- Bottom hand frames on the posted knee.
- Protect your neck if they grip the collar.

### Frames
- Hand or forearm on the posted knee pushes it away so you can bring your knee in under it.

### Priorities
1. Stop turning away.
2. Turn back flat toward their posted leg.
3. Frame on the posted knee.
4. Elbow-knee escape to half guard.

### Common mistakes
- **Turning to your stomach:** they take your back.
- **Extending the top arm to push them:** that's the armbar.
- **Staying on your side:** they settle and choose.
