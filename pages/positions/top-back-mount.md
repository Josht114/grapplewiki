Behind the opponent with hooks or a body triangle and a seatbelt grip. The highest-percentage submission position.

## Key points

1. Seatbelt grip with your choking arm over the shoulder.
2. Chest glued to their back; head beside theirs.
3. Hooks in, or lock a body triangle for more control.
4. Keep the choking hand hidden until you're ready to attack.

## Principles

### Goal
- Keep them attached and finish a choke.

### Base and posture
- Chest glued to their back, hips tight to theirs; no gap between you.
- Hooks inside their thighs with your heels in, or a body triangle.
- Your head beside theirs on the choking-arm side, pinning their head forward.

### Hands and arms
- Seatbelt: choking arm over the shoulder, other arm under the armpit, hands clasped at their chest.
- Hand-fight: two-on-one their defending hand to clear the way for the choke.
- Gi: collar grips for the bow and arrow and the sliding collar choke.

### Priorities
1. Seatbelt and hooks; keep their shoulders off the mat.
2. Follow their hips if they slide; re-hook or switch to mount rather than letting go.
3. Strip the defending hands, then rear naked choke or collar choke.
4. If they turn in, take mount.

### Common mistakes
- **Crossing your feet in front:** they lock a foot and break your ankle.
- **Gap at the chest:** they slide down and out.
- **Squeezing a choke that isn't under the chin:** you tire your arms out and give them time.
