---
name: Bottom side control
side: bottom
counterpart: top-side-control
---
Opponent is across your chest. Frame on the hip and neck, make space, and recover guard or come up to your knees.

## Key points

1. Frame one forearm on the neck, the other hand on the hip.
2. Don't push with straight arms; frames hold space, hips make it.
3. Turn in toward them; never give your back.
4. Bridge to make space, then shrimp and bring your knee in.

## Principles

### Goal
- Survive first, then recover guard or come up to your knees. Escape before you think about attacking.

### Base and posture
- Lie on your side facing them, not flat on your back.
- Feet on the mat, hips free to bridge and shrimp.
- Head turned toward them; if they've crossfaced you, getting it back is job one.

### Hands and arms
- Near arm: forearm blade across their neck or collarbone.
- Far arm: hand or forearm on their near hip.
- Elbows tight to your ribs; never reach across their body (kimura, americana, armbar).
- Gi: grab the belt or pants at the hip to frame. No-gi: palm or forearm on the hip bone.

### Frames
- Frames hold space; your hips make it. Bridge into them, then shrimp away into the gap the frames kept.
- Bend the arms at about 90 degrees, bone on bone. Straight arms collapse and get attacked.

### Priorities
1. Stop the crossface and far underhook: head and near elbow back in.
2. Set the neck and hip frames.
3. Bridge, shrimp, knee in: recover half or full guard.
4. If they block the knee, dig the near underhook and come up to your knees.

### Common mistakes
- **Pushing with straight arms:** you tire out and offer an armbar.
- **Turning away:** gives up your back.
- **Lying flat:** they settle their weight and you can't move your hips.
