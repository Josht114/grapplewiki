name: Bottom open guard
side: bottom
counterpart: top-open-guard
---
You are on your back with your legs between you and the opponent, who is either standing or kneeling. Manage distance with your feet and grips, and sweep when their posture breaks.

## Key points

1. Keep your feet and knees between you and them.
2. Against a standing passer: control distance with feet on hips, biceps or knees.
3. Against a kneeling passer: stay on your side and frame on the head and shoulders.
4. Sweep or attack when they lean or post a hand.

## Principles

### Goal
- Keep your legs between you and them, then sweep, submit or come up.

### Base and posture
- On your back or hip with your hips facing them; turn as they circle.
- Knees bent, feet active on their hips, biceps or knees.
- Chin tucked, head off the mat.

### Hands and arms
- Gi: sleeves, collar and pants.
- No-gi: wrists, collar tie, ankles and overhooks.
- Aim for two points of control, at least one with the feet.

### Frames
- Feet on the hips and shins on the body keep distance.
- Knees and elbows close together so they can't get inside.
- If they get past your feet, hip-escape and pummel a knee back in.

### Priorities
1. Distance and inside position: feet or knees between you and them.
2. Grips: two points of control.
3. Break their balance, then sweep, submit or wrestle up.
4. If they get halfway past, frame and re-guard.

### Common mistakes
- **Lying flat:** your hips can't follow them.
- **Straight or idle legs:** they pin them and pass.
- **No grips:** they control the pace and the angle.
