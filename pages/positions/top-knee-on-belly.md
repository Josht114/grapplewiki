Knee across the opponent's stomach with the other leg posted wide. A mobile pressure position between side control and mount.

## Key points

1. Slide your shin across their belly, toes toward the far hip.
2. Post the far leg wide for base.
3. Grip the far collar and the near pants or belt.
4. Ride their movement and step to mount when they push the knee.

## Principles

### Goal
- Make them uncomfortable enough to push or turn, then take what opens: mount, the back or the far arm.

### Base and posture
- Shin across their belly, knee on the far side of the belly button, toes of that foot on the mat.
- Other leg posted wide and out of reach; hips up and forward over the knee, chest up.
- Heavy on the knee while they're still; light and ready to follow when they move.

### Hands and arms
- Gi: near collar or lapel and the far knee or pants.
- No-gi: far shoulder or an underhook on the far arm, the other hand on the near hip.
- Keep your arms bent and attached to them; they steer you as much as they hold you.

### Priorities
1. Drive the knee and posture up; ride their hip escape rather than letting it slide off.
2. When they push your knee or chest, take the far-side armbar.
3. When they turn away, step to mount or take the back; when they turn in, go back to side control.

### Common mistakes
- **Leaning forward:** they push you over or roll you.
- **Posted leg too close:** they hook it and recover half guard.
- **Staying static:** the knee is a pressure position, not a pin to rest in.
