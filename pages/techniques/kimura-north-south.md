Figure-four shoulder lock on the near arm while you're in north south.

## Key steps

1. Trap their near arm off the mat.
2. Grip their wrist and thread your other arm under to grab your wrist.
3. Sit your hips back to keep their shoulder down.
4. Rotate the arm up toward their head.

## Detailed breakdown

### Start: north south
- Your chest is on theirs, head toward their hips, hips low behind their head.

### 1. Trap their near arm off the mat
- When their near arm comes up or reaches round you, catch it and keep it off the mat, elbow bent.

### 2. Grip their wrist and thread your other arm under to grab your wrist
- Grip their wrist with one hand; your other arm goes under their upper arm and grabs your own wrist: the kimura grip.

### 3. Sit your hips back to keep their shoulder down
- Sit back toward their head with your weight on their chest, pinning their shoulder blade to the mat.
- A pinned shoulder can't turn to escape the lock.

### 4. Rotate the arm up toward their head
- Keep their elbow bent at 90° and rotate their hand up toward their head and away from their body. Go slowly.

### Common mistakes
- **Letting their shoulder come off the mat:** they roll toward you and the lock goes.
- **Straightening their arm:** keep the elbow bent or the kimura has no lever.

## Defense

Grab your own belt or pants to stop the rotation, then turn into them.

1. Keep your near arm tight to your body.
2. Grab your belt, pants or gi.
3. Turn toward their legs to take away the angle.
