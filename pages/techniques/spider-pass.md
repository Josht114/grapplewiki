Breaking the sleeve grips of the spider guard and passing.

## Key steps

1. Circle one hand to break that sleeve grip.
2. Push the freed foot down to the mat.
3. Break the other sleeve grip the same way.
4. Pin their legs and step around to side control.

## Detailed breakdown

### Start: top of spider guard
- They grip both your sleeves, with their feet on your biceps, stretching your arms out. Gi only.

### 1. Circle one hand to break that sleeve grip
- Circle your hand inward or outward around their foot, turning your wrist against their thumb, and pull it free. Don't pull straight back; that tightens the grip.

### 2. Push the freed foot down to the mat
- Push the leg on that side down to the mat with your free hand, pinning it.

### 3. Break the other sleeve grip the same way
- Circle and strip the second grip, or push their other foot off your bicep.

### 4. Pin their legs and step around to side control
- With both legs pinned or pushed to one side, step around and drop into side control.

### Common mistakes
- **Pulling straight back against the grip:** you just stretch yourself. Turn the wrist.
- **Passing with one grip still on:** they swing into a triangle or lasso.

## Defense

Keep both sleeve grips tight and both feet pushing on their biceps, so their arms stay stretched and they can't break free.

1. Grip the sleeves at the cuff, pulling against your feet.
2. Keep your feet on their biceps, knees bent and pushing.
3. Re-grip straight away when they free an arm.
4. Switch to a lasso or triangle if they push a foot down.
