Posture up and stand to break the opponent's closed guard.

## Key steps

1. Establish posture with grips on their chest or belt.
2. Step one foot up, then the other.
3. Keep your back straight and hips forward.
4. Step back or drop a knee to break the locked ankles.

## Detailed breakdown

### Start: inside their closed guard, kneeling
- You have posture; their legs are locked.

### 1. Establish posture with grips on their chest or belt
- Grip their belt or press their chest, back straight, before you try to stand.

### 2. Step one foot up, then the other
- Bring one foot up beside their hip, then the other, standing up with them still locked on you.

### 3. Keep your back straight and hips forward
- Stand tall, hips pushed forward, so their weight hangs on their legs.

### 4. Step back or drop a knee to break the locked ankles
- Step one leg back, or drop a knee into their butt, so their legs stretch and the ankles open.

### Common mistakes
- **Standing with your back bent:** they pull your head down or sweep you.

## Defense

Keep your ankles locked and break their posture as they stand.

1. Pull them forward with sleeve or collar grips.
2. Keep your ankles locked tight.
3. Switch to an open guard sweep if the legs open.
