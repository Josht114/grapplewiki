Figure-four shoulder lock with the opponent's arm bent palm-up beside their head.

## Key steps

1. Pin the far wrist to the mat with the elbow bent at 90°.
2. Thread your other arm under their elbow and grab your own wrist.
3. Keep the wrist on the mat and slide it toward their hip.
4. Lift the elbow to finish.

## Detailed breakdown

### Start: side control
- You're chest to chest across them, hips low, with a crossface or head control.

### 1. Pin the far wrist to the mat with the elbow bent at 90°
- Their far arm is up beside their head. Pin the wrist to the mat with your hand on that side, their elbow bent like an L.
- Use your bodyweight on the wrist, not just arm strength.

### 2. Thread your other arm under their elbow and grab your own wrist
- Slide your other hand under their upper arm, near the elbow, and grab your own wrist: a figure-four.
- Keep your elbow tight to their head so they can't slip the arm out.

### 3. Keep the wrist on the mat and slide it toward their hip
- Paint the back of their hand along the mat toward their hip, like a brush. It mustn't leave the mat.

### 4. Lift the elbow to finish
- Lift their elbow slightly off the mat while the wrist stays down; the shoulder rotates and they tap.
- Go slowly: the americana comes on suddenly.

### Common mistakes
- **Letting the wrist come up:** the angle disappears. Keep it on the mat.
- **Elbow too far from their body:** slide toward the hip so the elbow stays close.

## Defense

Keep the elbow from being pinned and the wrist from being slid down; straighten or turn the arm in.

1. Keep your elbows close to your body, not above your head.
2. If the wrist is pinned, straighten the arm.
3. Turn into them to take away the angle.
4. Bridge to break their base.
